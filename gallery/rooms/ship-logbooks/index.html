Reports in each sampled month, drawn as pen strokes (logarithmic height). Click a stroke to open that period.

What you see. Each dot is one half-degree cell of ocean where a ship wrote a weather report in the sampled month. Dark ink is the current period. The ink of earlier periods fades slowly, so old routes give way to new ones. From 1750 to 1855 the colored lines are single voyages from the CLIWOC logbooks, drawn by nation as the year counter advances. The platform switch adds the buoys and automatic stations that take over the oceans after 1980.

What you hear. The wind is filtered noise. Its loudness and its pitch rise with the number of reports in the month. The rope creaks are short resonant bursts. Their rate follows the density of reports in the current view, and they are panned to where the ink falls. A soft ship bell rings when the counter enters a new century.

Sources: NOAA NCEI, ICOADS Release 3.1 (final untrimmed IMMA1 monthly files, 1662–2014), one sampled month per period, at most 20,000 reports per month.
