The Treasury Machine

Federal receipts and outlays, fiscal years 1789 to 2025, as a hydraulic machine. Income flows in from the left, sized to scale. Spending drains out to the right and splits into programs. The tank in the middle is the balance sheet: red below the zero line is debt held by the public. Every width and every level is in percent of GDP.

Reading the machine

Trade surplus is not revenue. The trade balance belongs to the national accounts. The only trade money the Treasury receives is customs duties, so that is the stream shown.

Interest flows both ways. Net interest on the debt is an outlay on the right. Interest the government earns, and Federal Reserve remittances, are small streams inside "Other receipts" on the left.

The tank is debt held by the public. From 1940 a lighter band on top shows debt held by government accounts, mostly the Social Security trust funds. Before 1940 the Treasury series is gross debt, and the two were nearly the same.

Programs appear when the data splits them. Receipts by source start in 1934, outlays by function in 1940, and by program in 1962. Open a spending function to see a third layer of details from 1962. Before those years the machine shows the Census Bureau series: customs, internal revenue, Army, Navy, interest, pensions.

Seams. GDP before 1940 is a calendar-year estimate (MeasuringWorth). The fiscal year ran July to June until 1976. New Deal relief sits inside "Everything else" until 1940, because the function split starts in 1940.

Sound. Each fiscal year plays one note. The note falls as the debt rises and climbs as the debt falls. A low splash marks a deficit, and a bigger deficit makes a heavier splash. A bright chime marks a surplus. Water noise follows the flows: receipts on the left, outlays on the right. A bell rings at each event on the timeline. Drag the year slider to play the notes by hand.
